Assignments & Lab Evaluations

Assignments and practical examinations vary slightly depending on the course and faculty, so it's always a good idea to confirm the required format beforehand. Keeping track of deadlines and guidelines is key to securing continuous internal marks.

Written Assignments

Type: Continuous Internal Assessment Target: Core Subjects

Different faculty members may have different expectations regarding formatting, submission methods, and evaluation criteria. When in doubt, ask!

Be sure to keep track of deadlines and structure write-ups according to the guidelines shared. In most courses, these submissions are compiled in standard assignment blue books and contribute directly to the 10-mark continuous assessment block.

Physics Laboratory Evaluation

Duration: Weekly Session Component: Lab Exam + Viva

The practical exam generally involves performing experiments, recording observations and results, and answering viva questions.

Ensure that your lab record and observation registers are signed every week by the course instructors. The final practical grading is based on experimental accuracy, formatting of result logs, and viva performance.

Chemistry Laboratory Evaluation

Record: Observation Registry Accessory: ChemSketch Diagrams

The assessment usually includes writing a detailed lab report, preparing a ChemSketch diagram (if required), and answering Viva questions.

Keep your chemical formulas, chemical reactions, and calculation charts neat. Be prepared to answer questions on indicators, titrations, and chemical properties during the viva voce.

Programming Labs (Python / C)

Platform: Compiler Environment Pattern: Timed Exams & Project Demos

During the first year, programming courses (such as Python and C) typically include two practical lab examinations in the semester, one after each ISA. These exams assess your coding skills, logical thinking, and ability to solve programming problems within a given time.

Second Year and Onwards: The evaluation pattern changes for most coding-based courses. Instead of traditional lab exams, you'll usually be assessed through a combination of mini-projects, programming assignments, coding challenges, demonstrations, presentations, and periodic submissions. Check the course plan at the start of the semester for exact schemes.
